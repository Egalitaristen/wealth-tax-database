## 2. Wealth tax history and live proposals (2010 onwards)
- Nigeria has **no federal annual net wealth tax** and **no inheritance or estate duty**: the Capital Transfer Tax Act (Decree No. 12 of 1979) was abrogated in the 1996 budget and dropped from the Laws of the Federation of Nigeria 2004 ([Taxing the Rich: Nigerian Fair Tax Monitor Thematic Report, Oct 2024](https://oi-files-cng-v2-prod.s3.eu-west-2.amazonaws.com/nigeria.oxfam.org/s3fs-public/Taxing%20the%20Rich%20Spread.pdf?VersionId=0nArswGanmdZOgSm4gvshQZvXqKBUU_8)).
- **Nigeria Tax Act, 2025** (enacted 26 June 2025, taking effect January 2026) overhauled income tax, capital gains tax, VAT and development levies, raising the top Personal Income Tax marginal rate to 25% without introducing a net wealth tax or an inheritance/estate tax ([PLAC summary of the Tax Reform Bills](https://placng.org/Legist/understanding-the-tax-reform-bills/); [Leadership on Taiwo Oyedele's committee reforms](https://leadership.ng/inside-nigerias-new-tax-reform-system/)).
- **October 2024 Oxfam / CISLAC / TJNA / CARE Nigeria Inequality & Fair Tax Monitor Reports (`H0001`, `H0002`, `H0006`):** On 15 October 2024, Oxfam in Nigeria (`A0001`), CISLAC (`A0002`), Tax Justice and Governance Platform (`A0003`), CARE International in Nigeria (`A0012`), and Tax Justice Network Africa (`A0016`) published *Income and Wealth Inequality in Nigeria: Trends and Drivers* and *Taxing the Rich: Nigerian Fair Tax Monitor Thematic Report*, proposing a 1% annual net wealth tax above $1 million (raising ~$7.5bn/year) or tiered rates of 2% above $5m, 3%–5% above $50m and 5%–10% above $1bn (raising $2.4bn–$6.1bn/year), alongside an inheritance tax above ₦50 million and a Comprehensive Wealth Registry ([Inequality Study Policy Brief PDF](https://oi-files-cng-v2-prod.s3.eu-west-2.amazonaws.com/nigeria.oxfam.org/s3fs-public/POLICY%20BRIEF%20-%20The%20Inequality%20Study%20-%20Oxfam%20Nigeria%20%5BWEB%5D.pdf?VersionId=l6qTvy6zHRQvw5qVoERbhbP_kJCD2DVe); [Taxing the Rich PDF](https://oi-files-cng-v2-prod.s3.eu-west-2.amazonaws.com/nigeria.oxfam.org/s3fs-public/Taxing%20the%20Rich%20Spread.pdf?VersionId=0nArswGanmdZOgSm4gvshQZvXqKBUU_8); [Nigerian Tribune, 15 Oct 2024](https://tribuneonlineng.com/nigeria-can-generate-n4-59trn-from-taxing-4690-wealthiest-citizens-yearly-cislac/)).
- **January 2023 & January 2026 Davos Briefings in Abuja (`H0003`):** Oxfam in Nigeria (`A0001`) and Connected Development (`A0014`) unveiled Nigeria-specific wealth tax projections in Abuja in January 2023 (2%/3%/5% above $5m/$50m/$1bn raising $3.2bn/year) and January 2026 (1% on net worth above $1m) ([Vanguard, 16 Jan 2023](https://www.vanguardngr.com/2023/01/davos-2023-3-richest-men-in-nigeria-wealthier-than-83m-nigerians-oxfam/); [Vanguard, 20 Jan 2026](https://www.vanguardngr.com/2026/01/billionaire-wealth-jumps-3-times-faster-in-2025-to-18-3trn-oxfam/)).
- **February 2024 Lagos National Tax Conference (`H0004`):** Chartered Accountant Chief Blakey Okwudili Ijezie (`A0031`) convened *Wealth Taxation in Nigeria: A Veritable Tool for Sustainable Development* in Kosofe LGA, Lagos, with speakers Pastor Felix Jarikre (`A0032`) and Dr Tunji Adeniyi (`A0033`), calling for a federal Wealth Tax Law based on the net fair market value of assets ([Vanguard, 13 Mar 2024](https://www.vanguardngr.com/2024/03/blakeys-wealth-tax-conference/)).
- **December 2025 African Cities Research Consortium Lagos Study (`H0005`):** Four Nigerian academics (`A0037`–`A0040`) published an empirical study of Lagos State's Land Use Charge proposing stronger vacant land taxation, land-value capture mechanisms and closing exemptions exploited by wealthier landowners ([ACRC, 19 Dec 2025](https://www.african-cities.org/equity-and-digitisation-in-the-property-tax-system-in-lagos-a-win-win-for-all/)).
- **Polls:** No representative public opinion poll asking specifically about a net wealth tax or inheritance tax in Nigeria was found during R001.

## 3. Legislatures and where their records are
- **National Assembly (Senate and House of Representatives):** Bills, order papers and votes & proceedings are published by the Policy and Legal Advocacy Centre ([PLAC](https://placng.org/Legist/understanding-the-tax-reform-bills/)) and the National Assembly portal (`nass.gov.ng`). No standalone federal net wealth tax or estate duty bill has passed second reading since 2010.
- **State Houses of Assembly:** Lagos State House of Assembly and other state assemblies enact property taxes (Land Use Charge), which are out of the core wealth-tax definition unless specifically structured as vacant-land/land-value capture reforms targeting wealth concentration ([ACRC, 19 Dec 2025](https://www.african-cities.org/equity-and-digitisation-in-the-property-tax-system-in-lagos-a-win-win-for-all/)).

## 5. Government below national level
- Under Nigeria's 1999 Constitution, the federal government legislates on income, capital gains and stamp duties, while state internal revenue services (SIRS) collect personal income tax and state land charges ([PLAC](https://placng.org/Legist/understanding-the-tax-reform-bills/)).
- Local Government Areas (774 LGAs) are restricted by the Fourth Schedule of the 1999 Constitution to tenement rates, market/kiosk fees, motor park levies and radio/TV licences; they cannot enact a net wealth or inheritance tax ([Taxing the Rich PDF](https://oi-files-cng-v2-prod.s3.eu-west-2.amazonaws.com/nigeria.oxfam.org/s3fs-public/Taxing%20the%20Rich%20Spread.pdf?VersionId=0nArswGanmdZOgSm4gvshQZvXqKBUU_8)). Local sampling across 12 LGAs in R001 located national, state and local actors in their HQ/university LGAs (`Abuja Municipal`, `Ikeja`, `Kosofe`, `Lagos Mainland`, `Eti-Osa`, `Ibadan North`, `Ife Central`, `Akure South`) and confirmed that LGA councils themselves do not pass wealth-tax motions.
