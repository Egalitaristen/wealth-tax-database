## 2. Wealth tax history and live proposals (2010 onwards)
- **Historical context**: Singapore previously had an estate duty introduced in 1929, which was abolished on 15 February 2008 by then-Finance Minister Tharman Shanmugaratnam to encourage high-net-worth individuals and family offices to site capital in Singapore. There is no capital gains tax, dividend tax, or general wealth tax.
- **2019 Budget debate**: Cheryl Chan (People's Action Party MP for Fengshan SMC) formally questioned the government in Parliament whether Singapore should implement a net wealth tax and inheritance tax targeting the top 1%-2% ultra-wealthy individuals to fund long-term social needs ([Hansard Debate 9465](https://www.sgparliament.com/debate/9465)).
- **2020 General Election**: The Workers' Party (WP) included an explicit net wealth tax in its election manifesto *Working for Singapore*, calling for a 0.5% to 2% net wealth tax on the assets of the top 1% of wealth holders ([WP Manifesto](https://www.wp.sg/manifesto)).
- **2021 Parliamentary Adjournment Motion**: WP MP Assoc Prof Jamus Lim delivered a dedicated adjournment motion on 1 November 2021 titled *"Taxation for a Dynamic and Fair 21st Century Economy"*, proposing a tiered net wealth tax of 0.5% above S$10 million, 1% above S$50 million, and 2% above S$1 billion ([Mothership report](https://mothership.sg/2021/11/jamus-lim-wealth-tax-adjournment-motion/)).
- **2022-2026 Budget Debates**: During successive national Budget debates (particularly in opposition to raising the Goods and Services Tax from 7% to 9%), Leader of the Opposition Pritam Singh and MP Louis Chua pressed the government on wealth taxation, pointing to Swiss and Norwegian models ([WP Budget 2022 speech](https://www.wp.sg/parliament/budget-2022-charting-our-new-way-forward-together--speech-by-lo-pritam-singh), [WP Budget 2026 speech](https://www.wp.sg/parliament/speech-by-louis-chua-on-budget-2026)).
- **Opposition Party Manifestos**: The Progress Singapore Party (PSP) and Red Dot United (RDU) both formally integrated wealth taxation into their election platforms and Shadow Budgets as progressive alternatives to regressive consumption taxes ([CNA GE2025 PSP report](https://www.channelnewsasia.com/singapore/ge2025-progress-singapore-party-manifesto-leong-mun-wai-tan-cheng-bock-hazel-poa-5047561), [RDU Shadow Budget 2024](https://reddotunited.sg/latest/f/shadow-budget-2024---a-wellbeing-economy-for-shared-prosperity)).

## 4. Parties
- **The Workers' Party (WP)**: The primary parliamentary opposition party; official policy advocates a 0.5% to 2% net wealth tax on the top 1% wealth holders.
- **Progress Singapore Party (PSP)**: Proposes wealth taxes and estate duties to fund utility rebates and social support without increasing GST.
- **Red Dot United (RDU)**: Shadow Budgets (2024, 2025) call for introducing a wealth tax and raising top-1% income tax rates.
- **Singapore Democratic Party (SDP)**: Proposes luxury taxes and reinstating estate duty, but excluded from net wealth tax under Section 2 definitions unless explicit net wealth proposals are introduced.
- **People's Action Party (PAP)**: Ruling party; maintains property taxes and luxury vehicle taxes as proxy wealth taxes, but ministers (Lawrence Wong, Indranee Rajah) argue broad net wealth taxes pose capital-flight risks. Cheryl Chan (PAP MP) raised consideration of a net wealth tax on the top 1%-2% in 2019.

## 5. Government below national level
- **Structure**: Singapore operates as a unitary city-state with no provincial, state, or municipal legislatures. Local administration is organised via 5 Community Development Councils (CDCs: Central Singapore, North East, North West, South East, South West) and 17 Town Councils for estate management, neither of which has independent taxation powers.
- **Statistical / Planning Tier**: The Urban Redevelopment Authority (URA) divides the country into 5 Regions and 55 Planning Areas, which are the standard geographic units for the national census (SingStat).
- **Map & Geography Register**: Boundaries are sourced from URA via geoBoundaries gbOpen under the Singapore Open Data Licence Version 1.0 (an open government licence).
